Privacy policy

Garden Book (Android app, package io.github.teatimecrumbles.gardenbook) · Effective 14 November 2026

Summary

Garden Book sends nothing about you to the developer. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or trackers. Your garden settings stay on your phone. The app uses the internet only for Google Play purchases and for plant photos from Wikipedia, which it shows whenever the phone is online, unless you switch them off. If you buy the full book, Google gives the developer the order details described under “Buying the full book”.

Who makes the app

Garden Book is made by Mohammad Motiur Rahman, an independent developer who publishes apps as Teatime Crumbles. Contact: mohammadmotiurrahman@gmail.com

What stays on your phone

Your town, your frost-date adjustments, your units, the plants you save to My plants, the page you were reading and your display settings (page turn, wind, sound) are kept only on your phone, in the app’s own storage. So is a note of whether you have bought the full book and its price in your currency, and photos the app has shown may stay for a while in its cache. They are deleted when you clear the app’s storage in Android settings or uninstall the app.

The app never reads your location. When you first open it, it guesses your town from the time zone set on your phone, on the phone itself; you can then search for your town in the list built into the app. Nothing about your town is sent anywhere.

Android cloud backup is switched off for this app, so this information is not copied to your Google account. If you move to a new phone with Android’s device-to-device transfer, your settings and My plants can be copied directly to the new phone. Nothing is sent to the developer.

Buying the full book

The full book is a one-time purchase made through Google Play. Google Play shows the price, takes the payment and keeps the record of the purchase in your Google account, under Google’s privacy policy. The app and the developer never see your card or other payment details. The app only asks Google Play for the price and whether the full book has been bought on your Google account, so that it can open the pages and bring the purchase back with Restore purchase.

The app contains Google Play’s billing component, which talks to the Play Store app on your phone each time the app opens or comes back to the screen (to check the price and whether the book has been bought), and when you tap “Unlock the full book” or “Restore purchase”. It may send Google technical information about these requests, for running Google Play billing.

For each sale, Google gives the developer the order details that Google includes in its sales reports, such as the order number, the date, the price and taxes, the device model and the country of purchase (for some countries also the city, state or province and postal code). The developer uses them only for accounting, taxes and questions about an order, does not combine them with any other data, and keeps them only as long as accounting and tax rules require.

If you write to the developer, for example about a refund, your e-mail address and your message are used only to answer you, and are deleted when you ask.

Plant photos from Wikipedia

Each plant page has a drawing. If the phone is online, the app also shows a photo of the plant from Wikipedia when you open its page. Photos are on until you switch them off: the camera button on a page switches photos on or off, and the app remembers your choice. When you open the app, it checks once whether Wikipedia can be reached. To show a photo, the app asks Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org) for the page about the plant’s botanical name, for example “Mangifera indica”, then for the photographer and licence of that page’s picture, and loads the picture from Wikimedia Commons (upload.wikimedia.org). Only freely licensed pictures from Wikimedia Commons are shown.

These requests contain only the plant’s name and the name of its picture. Like any visit to a website, they reach the Wikimedia Foundation with your phone’s internet address and basic browser information, which the Wikimedia Foundation handles under its own privacy policy. The developer does not receive them. If you switch photos off, or the phone is offline, the app shows its drawings and makes no request to Wikipedia except the one check when it opens.

The credit under a photo (photographer and licence) opens the picture’s page on Wikimedia Commons in your web browser, where Wikimedia’s own privacy policy applies. The app also tells Android System WebView, which displays the app’s pages, not to send usage statistics about this app.

Permissions

The app has internet access (for purchases and photos) and Google Play’s billing permission, and it may check whether the phone is online. It asks for no other permissions: no camera, contacts, location, microphone, phone, photos or storage access.

Security

Your town, your settings and My plants are kept in the app’s private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read. The app allows only encrypted (HTTPS) connections: its pages can reach only en.wikipedia.org and upload.wikimedia.org, for the photos, and Google Play’s billing component talks to Google. The developer has no server for the app and holds no copy of your settings or My plants. The app saves no files to folders such as Pictures or Downloads, and it has no share button.

Children

Garden Book is made for adults who garden and is not directed at children. The app sends nothing to the developer about anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

The app sends nothing about you to the developer. If you buy the full book, the developer has only the order details from Google described above, kept only as long as accounting and tax rules require, and any e-mails you send. Everything the app stores is on your phone and under your control: to delete it, clear the app’s storage in Android settings or uninstall the app. To ask about an order or a refund, or to have your e-mails deleted, write to the address below.
