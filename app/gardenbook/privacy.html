Privacy policy

Garden Book (Android app, package io.github.teatimecrumbles.gardenbook) · Effective 14 November 2026

Summary

Garden Book does not collect, share or sell any personal data. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or trackers. Your garden settings stay on your phone. The app uses the internet only for Google Play purchases and, if you want them, plant photos from Wikipedia.

Who makes the app

Garden Book is made by Mohammad Motiur Rahman, an independent developer who publishes apps as Teatime Crumbles. Contact: mohammadmotiurrahman@gmail.com

What stays on your phone

Your town, your frost-date adjustments, your units, the plants you save to My plants, the page you were reading and your display settings (page turn, wind, sound) are kept only on your phone, in the app’s own storage. So is a note of whether you have bought the full book and its price in your currency. They are deleted when you clear the app’s storage in Android settings or uninstall the app.

The app never reads your location. When you first open it, it guesses your town from the time zone set on your phone, on the phone itself; you can then search for your town in the list built into the app. Nothing about your town is sent anywhere.

Android cloud backup is switched off for this app, so this information is not copied to your Google account. If you move to a new phone with Android’s device-to-device transfer, your settings and My plants can be copied directly to the new phone. Nothing is sent to the developer.

Buying the full book

The full book is a one-time purchase made through Google Play. Google Play shows the price, takes the payment and keeps the record of the purchase in your Google account, under Google’s privacy policy. The app never sees your card or payment details. It only asks Google Play whether the full book has been bought on your Google account, so that it can open the pages and bring the purchase back with Restore purchase.

The app contains Google Play’s billing component, which talks to the Play Store app on your phone. Google may receive technical information about purchase requests from it for running Google Play billing. As the seller, the developer receives from Google the order details Google gives every Google Play seller (for example the order number, date, country and amount), to handle refunds, taxes and questions you send. The developer does not use them for anything else.

Plant photos from Wikipedia

Each plant page has a drawing. If the phone is online, the app can also show a photo of the plant from Wikipedia: the camera button on a page switches photos on or off, and the app remembers your choice. When you open the app, it checks once whether Wikipedia can be reached. To show a photo, the app asks Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org) for the page about the plant’s botanical name, for example “Mangifera indica”, and then loads the picture from Wikimedia (upload.wikimedia.org).

These requests contain only the plant’s name. Like any visit to a website, they reach the Wikimedia Foundation with your phone’s internet address and basic browser information, which the Wikimedia Foundation handles under its own privacy policy. The developer does not receive them. If you leave photos switched off, or the phone is offline, the app shows its drawings and makes no request to Wikipedia except the one check when it opens.

The “Photo: Wikipedia” link under a photo opens the Wikipedia page in your web browser, where Wikipedia’s own privacy policy applies. The app also tells Android System WebView, which displays the app’s pages, not to send usage statistics about this app.

Permissions

The app has internet access (for purchases and photos) and Google Play’s billing permission, and it may check whether the phone is online. It asks for no other permissions: no camera, contacts, location, microphone, phone, photos or storage access.

Children

Garden Book is made for adults who garden and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

The app sends nothing about you to the developer, so the developer holds no data about you to delete, apart from the Google Play order details described above, which Google keeps. Everything the app stores is on your phone and under your control. To ask about an order or a refund, write to the address below.
